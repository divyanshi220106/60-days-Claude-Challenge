# Day 32 — Think Like a Marketing Strategist: Grow This Brand

## What I Built
A single-file, offline-capable React app that teaches marketing **strategy**, not just marketing content generation. Instead of spitting out captions, it walks the user through the actual decisions a strategist makes: choosing a brand mode, understanding the audience, picking platforms, defining content pillars, planning a 30-day roadmap, and reacting to a randomized real-world curveball — ending in a personalized Growth Report.

**Stack:** React 18 (CDN) + Babel Standalone (CDN, classic JSX runtime) + vanilla CSS. No Tailwind, no npm build step, no backend, no external APIs. Runs entirely client-side from a single `.html` file.

## The Flow
1. **Welcome** — frames the "why" of strategic thinking over content generation
2. **Choose Your Path** — Use My Own Business / Build My Personal Brand / A New Client Has Arrived (randomly generated)
3. **Know Your Audience** — form inputs or an auto-generated client brief, plus a derived audience insight
4. **Platform Selection** — 8 platforms, each tagged Strong / Situational / Weak fit, with reasoning that changes for business vs. personal brand mode
5. **Content Pillars** — 6 options per mode, forced pick-exactly-3 to teach focus over breadth
6. **30-Day Roadmap** — 4 weekly strategic goals (not a post calendar)
7. **Curveball Event** — a randomized unexpected scenario (viral post, price war, PR complaint, podcast invite, copycat, follower spike) with 3 response choices and real consequences
8. **Growth Report** — a computed Growth Potential score, Best Decision, Biggest Mistake, and 3 marketing lessons, all derived from the actual choices made during the run
9. **"How to Ask Claude" prompt cards** after every major section — reusable, personalized prompts so the tool also teaches prompt engineering alongside marketing

## Key Learnings

**1. Teaching strategy requires forcing tradeoffs, not just presenting options.**
The pick-exactly-3-pillars constraint and the platform fit tags (Strong/Situational/Weak) do more pedagogical work than any explanation text — they make the user feel the cost of spreading effort too thin.

**2. A believable simulation needs branching consequences, not just branching choices.**
The curveball event only "teaches" something if the wrong answer visibly costs something (trust, momentum, budget) and the report reflects that back. Static content would have felt like a form, not a simulation.

**3. Small CDN version drift can silently break a whole app.**
Loading `@babel/standalone` without a pinned version pulled in a build whose default JSX transform emits `import` statements — which a classic `<script>` tag can't execute. Pinning to `@babel/standalone@7.24.7` (classic runtime) fixed it. Lesson: always pin CDN dependency versions in anything meant to "just work" when someone else opens it.

**4. Personal-brand marketing and business marketing are structurally different, not just reskinned.**
Reusing the same platform/pillar/event logic for both modes required real differentiation (e.g., LinkedIn/X/YouTube/Newsletter weighted higher for personal brands, "competitors" reframed as "people in your space you admire") — a good reminder that good UX copy is a design decision, not an afterthought.
