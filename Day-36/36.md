# Day 36 — Cognitive Pattern Explorer

## 🎯 Objective
Build and play a calm, game-like self-reflection experience that explores everyday thinking tendencies — without ever diagnosing or clinically labeling anything.

## 🛠️ What I Built
A single-file, offline HTML/CSS/vanilla-JS app: **Cognitive Pattern Explorer**.

- **Modes:** Calm Mode (slow pacing, soft cool palette) and Stress Mode (quicker pacing, warmer palette) — same content, different emotional texture.
- **Chapter 1 — Discover Your Thinking Style:** six everyday scenarios (a last-minute plan change, an ambiguous text, a job decision, an unexpected obstacle, a big purchase, unexpected feedback), each with five response options mapped to a thinking tendency.
- **Chapter 2 — Choose Your Priorities:** five draggable cards ranked from "most like me" to "least like me," with mouse drag, touch drag, and keyboard (↑/↓ buttons + arrow keys) support.
- **Chapter 3 — Map Your Thinking:** a draggable timeline where I arranged five thinking steps into the order I'd naturally move through them when facing a decision.
- **Reflection Journal:** a percentage breakdown across all five tendencies, a leading-tendency callout, a per-chapter comparison (which tendency stood out in each chapter), and a personalized reflective insight written in "you often... this suggests..." language — never an absolute label.

### The five thinking tendencies
1. Analytical Thinker
2. Emotional Intuitive
3. Overthinking Loop Style
4. Action-First Decision Maker
5. Balanced Reflective Thinker

## 🧠 Key Learnings

1. **Reflective language changes how a result feels.** Swapping "You are an Overthinker" for "You often revisit decisions from multiple angles — this suggests a thorough style" turns a label into an invitation to notice a pattern, not an identity to accept or reject.
2. **The same content, framed differently, produces different self-reports.** Playing in Calm Mode vs. Stress Mode with identical scenarios nudged my choices — under the quicker pacing I leaned more Action-First and Overthinking-Loop; in Calm Mode I leaned more Balanced and Analytical. Pace and pressure shape decision style, not just content.
3. **Ranking reveals more than multiple choice.** Chapter 1's scenario picks and Chapter 2's priority ranking didn't always agree — comparing "what I say I'd do" against "what I rank as closest to me" surfaced a gap worth noticing.
4. **Sequence matters as much as preference.** Chapter 3's timeline (arranging *when* each thinking mode kicks in, not just which one dominates) showed that most of my tendencies aren't mutually exclusive — I move through several in sequence rather than living in just one.
5. **Accessibility and calm design aren't at odds.** Building keyboard-operable drag-and-drop (arrow keys and up/down buttons alongside native drag/touch) alongside `prefers-reduced-motion` support made the experience usable without weakening the calm, ambient aesthetic.
6. **A percentage breakdown is more honest than a single label.** Presenting all five tendencies as a spread, rather than crowning one "type," better reflects that thinking style is a mixture that shifts with context — which the tool explicitly reminds the user of in its closing disclaimer.
